# Description

## Introduction

Industrial systems are prone to defects and failures that may lead to various undesirable consequences. Therefore, industrial practitioners and managers are motivated to leverage real-time system monitoring techniques to mitigate the risk of failures, reduce machinery downtime, and achieve optimum productivity by performing efficient maintenance strategies.

Vibration signals are widely used for fault detection in industrial/manufacturing machinery and are the focus of this project, which is based on the data challenge of the 2023 IEEE International Conference on Prognostics and Health Management. The project uses a subset of the data for this competition, and involves records on vibration signals and failures of a specific component that generates them.

## Data Generation

The data was generated using a test rig consisting of a driving motor, two-stage planetary gearbox, two-stage parallel gearbox, and magnetic brake. The test rig is shown below.

Essentially, the test rig is driven by a motor (2) at a specific rotating speed (in rpm), which may be adjusted by the speed transducer (8). A torque is applied to the output shaft of the parallel gearbox (5) by the magnetic brake (6). The acceleration sensor (7), mounted on the second stage of the planetary gearbox (4), generates vibration signals that are stored using a portable data acquisition system (1). Each vibration signal is recorded for a period of five minutes, with a sampling frequency of 10 kHz.

Due to the heavy alternative stress and impact, sun gear teeth on the second stage of the planetary gearbox (4) are more likely to suffer damage in practical application. A common sun gear fault is damage to its surface. The pictures below show sun gears with no damage and with damage in their surface wear.

If the sun gear has damage, the vibration signals generated by the portable data acquisition system look slightly different from those obtained when the equipment was undamaged.

## Goal

The goal of this project is to build a classification algorithm that identifies whether a sun gear has a fault using data from the vibration signals.

# Getting Started

You can download the training and test datasets by clicking on the Data tab. The data comes from real vibration signals.

To create your team, all team members must first create a Kaggle account. Then, you need to merge your accounts into a team. Click on the Team tab at the top. From there, you can search for the usernames of your team members and request a merge.

## Submit Predictions

Once you are confident about the classification for all signals in the test set, you must submit a comma-separated values (CSV) file with two columns. The first column is called id and should be the same as the id column in the test.csv file. The second column is called class and must contain the predicted classes (not probabilities): 0 for undamaged and 1 for damaged. The predictor columns and their values must be removed from the submission file.

An example of a correct submission file is sample.csv located at the Data tab.

## Public and Private Leaderboards

You can see the model performance under the Leaderboard tab. To provide an accurate assessment of a model's performance, Kaggle has two leaderboards. The public leaderboard is the one you see under the Leaderboard tab and shows the performance of your model on 60% of the test dataset. The private leaderboard shows the performance of the model on the other 40% of the test data, and it is only available to the host. The private leaderboard will be available to all teams once the competition ends.

## Model Score

The actual accuracy value of your model used to determine your Kaggle score is not that displayed on the public leaderboard under the Leaderboard tab. Your Kaggle score will be determined using the private leaderboard. The results on the private leaderboard are calculated similarly to those on the public leaderboard. However, the private results use a private, set-aside portion of the test dataset. Therefore, your model must not only perform well on the public test dataset, but it must also generalize to the private test dataset.

The evaluation metric for this competition is the Accuracy on the test data. High values for this metric are preferred.

To calculate the Kaggle score of a team, we will use five benchmark models called Iron Man, Wasp, Star-Lord, Spider-Man, and Captain Marvel. The Kaggle score will be calculated in terms of how many models you beat on the private leaderboard; see the "Getting Started" section.

The Kaggle score ranges from 0 to 30. The score you get by beating the benchmark models is

12 If you outperform two benchmark models on the private leaderboard.
18 If you outperform three benchmark models on the private leaderboard.
24 If you outperform four benchmark models on the private leaderboard.
30 If you outperform all benchmark models on the private leaderboard.
